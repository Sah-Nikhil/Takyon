# v0.17 Time zones

Time zone conversion in the calculator Source: `12 pm utc` answers with the
local time, `3:30pm pst to ist` converts between zones, and `time in tokyo`
shows the time there now. All of it offline, from tzdb data that ships with the
binary or the OS.

**Driver: asked for by name** (2026-10-07). `12 pm utc` in the Palette yields no
row. v0.4 built unit conversion for length, mass, temperature, data and duration,
and never planned time zones; no doc mentioned them until this one. Duration
(`90 min to h`) already works and is untouched here: it is a `Dim::Time` unit,
and a time of day is not a unit.

**Status: built 2026-10-07; the verify script is unrun.** Not a v1.0 blocker,
and independent of v0.12 to v0.16. The build changed two parts of the design
below; § What the build changed says which, and wins where they disagree.

## Read first

- [ADR-0002](../adr/0002-bangless-queries-never-touch-the-network.md): the
  Bangless path never touches the network. Time zones are safe here because the
  rules are data on disk, unlike currency, whose rates are not.
- [ADR-0026](../adr/0026-objc2-is-the-macos-binding.md): the precedent of
  taking a crate `Cargo.lock` already holds rather than adding one. `jiff` 0.2
  and `jiff-tzdb-platform` are already in the lockfile, so a direct dependency
  on `jiff` adds no entry to it.
- [TBC-0011](../tbc/0011-hand-rolled-expression-parser.md): the parser is ours
  and hand-rolled. Time zones extend it rather than replacing it.
- `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/sources/calc/mod.rs`, the module doc: that file is
  policy, and policy is the hard part. A Calc Entry wins its tier outright, so a
  wrong time zone answer takes the top row and Enter with it.

## Design

### Grammar

| Input | Answer |
|---|---|
| `12 pm utc` | that instant in the local zone |
| `12pm utc to ist`, `12:00 utc in kolkata` | that instant in the target zone |
| `3:30pm pst to tokyo` | city names resolve, see § Zone names |
| `noon london to new york`, `midnight utc` | `noon` and `midnight` are times |
| `time in tokyo`, `tokyo time`, `now in utc` | now, in that zone |
| `14:00 utc+5:30 to utc` | numeric offsets on either side |

A time with no date means today in the **source** zone. When the conversion
crosses midnight, the answer says so (`Thu`, or `+1 day`), because `11 pm pst to
ist` with the day hidden reads as the wrong evening.

What never answers on the Bangless path in `Policy::Automatic`:

- **A bare time** (`12 pm`, `9:30`). There is nothing to convert, and `9:30` would
  otherwise beat every app with a version number in its title.
- **A bare zone or city** (`tokyo`, `ist`). An app, a file or a folder called
  Tokyo must keep the top row. Only `time in X`, `X time` and `now in X` ask
  for the current time.

`Policy::Explicit` keeps its meaning: nothing without the `=` prefix.

### Zone names

Three tables, consulted in this order:

1. **IANA ids**, case-insensitive: `asia/kolkata`, `america/new_york`. Always
   unambiguous.
2. **Cities**, derived from the last segment of every IANA id with `_` read as a
   space: `tokyo`, `new york`, `kolkata`. Plus a short hand-written alias list
   for cities tzdb does not name (`mumbai`, `delhi`, `san francisco`, `beijing`).
3. **Abbreviations**, hand-written and short: `utc`, `gmt`, `z` are fixed; the
   rest name a zone, not an offset. `pst` means `America/Los_Angeles`, so
   `12 pm pst` in July is read as Pacific time, and the answer labels it `PDT`.
   That is what people mean when they write `pst` in summer.

Abbreviations collide. `ist` is India, Israel and Ireland; `cst` is China and
US Central; `bst` is British Summer and Bangladesh. **One fixed reading each**,
listed in `tz.rs`: `ist` India, `cst` US Central, `bst` London (decided
2026-10-07). The subtitle spells the zone out in words (§ Answer shape), so a
wrong guess is visible before Enter.

### Answer shape

Every time zone answer names its zone three ways: the **code** in the title, and
the **place** and the **zone's name in words** with its UTC offset in the
subtitle. An IANA id like `Asia/Kolkata` is never shown: it is precise but not
readable. `time in london` must answer "it is 3:42 PM, which is BST, British
Summer Time, one hour ahead of UTC", not just a clock.

| Input | Title (copied on Enter) | Subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| `time in london` | `3:42 PM BST` | `London · British Summer Time, UTC+1 · Wed 7 Oct` |
| `time in london` in January | `2:42 PM GMT` | `London · Greenwich Mean Time, UTC+0 · Wed 7 Jan` |
| `12 pm utc to ist` | `5:30 PM IST` | `12:00 PM UTC → Kolkata · India Standard Time, UTC+5:30 · Wed 7 Oct` |
| `11 pm pst to ist` | `Thu 12:30 PM IST` | `11:00 PM PDT → Kolkata · India Standard Time, UTC+5:30 · Thu 8 Oct` |
| `12 pm utc` | `5:30 PM IST` (local) | `12:00 PM UTC → your time · India Standard Time, UTC+5:30 · Wed 7 Oct` |

- **The code** is the one in force at that instant, so London reads `BST` in
  summer and `GMT` in winter, and `pst` typed in July comes back `PDT`.
- **The place** is the city the user typed, or the last segment of the IANA id
  with `_` read as a space (`New York`). The local zone reads `your time`.
- **The name in words** comes from a hand-written table in `tz.rs`, keyed by
  zone **and** code, because the codes collide: tzdb gives `IST` to Kolkata,
  Dublin and Jerusalem, and they are India Standard, Irish Standard and Israel
  Standard Time. About forty rows cover every zone people name by code.
- **No name exists** for many zones: tzdb's code there is numeric (`+04` for
  Dubai). Then the title uses `UTC+4` and the subtitle drops the name rather
  than inventing one: `Dubai · UTC+4 · Wed 7 Oct`.
- The subtitle is long. It leads with what disambiguates (place, then name) so
  when a narrow Palette truncates it, the date goes first.
- 12-hour or 24-hour follows the Windows short-time format, read once at
  startup. A user who runs 24-hour clocks should not get `PM` from us.

### Where it lives

A new `sources/calc/tz.rs`, beside `units.rs`, with the same shape: `split()`
returns a parsed conversion or `None`, and `mod.rs` decides policy. `answer()`
tries units first, then time zones, then arithmetic: `pm` and `utc` are not
units, so a units miss costs one table lookup.

`answer()` gains an `answer_at(input, policy, now)` seam, as `Pipeline::query`
has `query_at`. Every test of "today" or "now" fixes the instant; a test that
reads the wall clock fails on the day DST changes.

### Data and the local zone

`jiff` with its default features. On Windows it bundles tzdb into the binary
(`jiff-tzdb-platform`) and maps the Windows zone id to IANA itself. On macOS it
reads `/usr/share/zoneinfo` and `/etc/localtime`, so v0.12 inherits this with no
`cfg` arm.

- The local zone is re-read **on Palette show**, not cached for the process
  lifetime. Takyon runs for weeks, and a laptop crosses zones. *Superseded:
  jiff's own 5-minute cache does this; § What the build changed.*
- Zones are parsed on first use and cached. The first `time in tokyo` must still
  meet the 30 ms first-Entry budget; if it does not, warm the cache in deferred
  init, behind the hotkey.
- The bundled tzdb goes stale. Windows ships tzdb updates through Windows Update
  and we would not see them; ours update only with a Takyon release. Recorded as
  a TBC with its trigger (a country changes its DST rules between releases) and
  the alternative: ICU's `ucal_*` from the OS's own `icu.dll`, which Windows
  updates, at the cost of FFI on Windows and a separate macOS path.

**Why `jiff` and not the OS or another crate.** Hand-rolling tzdb is not an
option. `chrono-tz` would be a new crate and has no system-zone support. The OS
route is zero crates but two implementations. `jiff` is MIT or Unlicense, already
locked, one implementation for both platforms, and handles DST gaps and folds
correctly. The ADR records this when the phase is approved.

## Traps

- **DST gaps and folds.** `2:30 am america/new_york` on 2026-03-08 does not
  exist, and `1:30 am` on 2026-11-01 happens twice. Use jiff's `compatible`
  disambiguation (gap: shift forward, fold: earlier) and test both dates.
- **`in` is inches.** `units::split` already tries ` to ` before ` in ` and
  splits at the first ` in `, so `5 ft in in` is a length. (`5 in in cm` never
  answered, before or after this phase.) Time zones run after units and only
  claim a query whose left side parses as a time of day.
- **`am` and `pm` without a space.** `12pm`, `12 pm`, `12:00pm`, `12 p.m.`.
- **`12 am` is midnight and `12 pm` is noon.** The classic off-by-twelve.
- **`m` is metres, `min` is minutes.** `12 m utc` must yield nothing, not noon.
- **Tests on a machine in any zone.** No test may depend on the machine's local
  zone. Inject it, like `now`.

## Tests

Rust unit tests, test-first, all at a fixed instant and a fixed local zone:

- every row of § Grammar, and each of the "never answers" cases
- `12 am` and `12 pm`, `noon` and `midnight`
- the DST gap and fold dates above, in a US and a European zone
- a conversion that crosses midnight forwards and one that crosses backwards
- each ambiguous abbreviation resolves to its fixed reading
- `time in london` reads `BST`, British Summer Time in July and `GMT`,
  Greenwich Mean Time in January; `time in dublin` reads Irish Standard Time,
  not India
- a zone with a numeric tzdb code shows `UTC+n` and no invented name
- every row of § Answer shape, verbatim
- `5 in to cm`, `5 ft in in` and `90 min to h` still answer as units
- `12 m utc` yields nothing

One visual baseline for a time zone row, through the existing mocked Calc Entry.
Nothing here can make a network request, so ADR-0002 needs no new test.

## Exit criteria

`12 pm utc` shows the local time, `3:30pm pst to ist` and `time in tokyo` answer
correctly on both sides of a DST change, `tokyo` alone still opens whatever is
called Tokyo, and the first-Entry budget holds.

## What the build changed

**The answer is a card, not a row with a subtitle.** § Answer shape was written
for a row. A Calc Entry has been drawn as a two-half card since v0.4.5
(`CalcCard.tsx`): the subtitle is the left half at 26 px, and the halves carry
the labels `Expression` and `Result`. A 60-character zone description in the
left half truncates to nothing useful. So a time zone card is:

| | Left half | Right half (copied on Enter) |
|---|---|---|
| Value | the time asked from: `12:00 PM UTC`, or for `time in X` the time here | the answer: `5:30 PM IST` |
| Label | `Coordinated Universal Time · Wed 7 Oct` | `Kolkata · India Standard Time, UTC+5:30 · Wed 7 Oct` |

That needed one new optional field on `Entry`, `labels: [string, string]`,
absent on every other Entry. `ipc.rs`'s contract list and `packages/shared` both
carry it. `labels` is also what tells `CalcCard` not to tint `+` and `-` as
operators, since `UTC+5:30` is not arithmetic. A `UTC` zone's label drops its
offset (`Coordinated Universal Time`, not `…, UTC+0`). Each label wraps to at
most two lines rather than truncating, and both halves reserve two so their
values stay level; the card's fixed height has room for that.

**No re-read on Palette show.** `jiff::tz::TimeZone::system()` caches the system
zone for 5 minutes and re-detects after that. A zone change reaches the Palette
within 5 minutes with no hook of ours. The plan's show hook would have been a
second cache over jiff's.

**Measured.** Release build on the development machine: 8 to 120 µs per time
zone query, the slowest being a city name, which scans tzdb's ~600 ids. Reading
the clock costs 0.4 µs. Nothing near the 30 ms budget, so nothing is warmed in
deferred init. The bundled tzdb is 202 KB before compression
(`jiff-tzdb`'s `concatenated-zoneinfo.dat`); the installer delta is not measured.
